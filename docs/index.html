AbstractWhat is in a routine EEG report, and can it be read from the signal?

Routine EEG guides the emergency care of seizures and altered consciousness, but expert reading is scarce and varies between readers. In 1,510 emergency patients, split at patient level, we compared a model on region-of-interest summary features of the whole recording with three deep networks on the raw 19-channel signal, for eight findings of the clinical report. All models were tested on held-out patients with complete-recording inference and then, without retraining, on three public datasets. The findings were recovered with AUROC between 0.68 and 0.90; explanations of the deep networks were tested for faithfulness, stability and dependence on what the network learned. These results measure agreement with a clinician’s report, not with a physiological ground truth.

2 · The models transfer to other hospitals, countries and recording systems.

Models applied unchanged to public datasets; thresholds and recalibration come from our validation patients only. NMT: 2,417 recordings (Pakistan). TUH: 2,993 recordings (USA). IFCN: epileptiform model against a clinical epilepsy diagnosis in 100 selected clips.

The ROI-feature model was not tested externally. Ranking ability transfers for all models; probabilities of the Conformers are more sensitive to the data they meet, as the next experiment shows.

3 · What transfer requires: the full spectrum.

The NMT recordings contain almost no power above about 35 Hz. We low-pass filtered 120 TUH records at 35 Hz and rescored them with the frozen models. Press the switch.

Bars: average probability that the recording is abnormal (a score level, not accuracy). Below each model, its AUROC, the ranking of recordings.

Band-limiting shifts the scores of the Conformers, which read the 0.5–45 Hz spectrum, while the ranking of recordings is unchanged. Thresholds are therefore set for the recording system in use, with recalibration on its own validation data. EEGNet, which reads the raw signal, keeps the same scores.

ScopeHow to read these results

  • The reference is the clinician’s report, a synthesis with measurement error: agreement with it is measured, not agreement with a physiological ground truth.
  • The test set has 302 patients, from one hospital and one period; confidence intervals are about ±0.04–0.05 wide, and almost all differences between models are within them.
  • External sets differ in population, labelling and recording; the IFCN reference is a clinical epilepsy diagnosis, not an annotation of epileptiform discharges.
  • Explanations describe the models, not causal physiology.

Data and codeAvailability

The code for the analysis is available on request. The clinical records and EEG contain sensitive patient information and are not public. The external datasets are public: TUH Abnormal EEG Corpus, NMT Scalp EEG Dataset and the IFCN sharp-transient clips (Kural et al., 2020).
